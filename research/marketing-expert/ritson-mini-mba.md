# Mark Ritson — distilled

> Marketing Week columns (2005–present) and the Mini MBA in Marketing (miniMBA.com). The working-strategist's case for strategy-before-execution rigour in marketing planning.

## Why it matters for AI-coding

AI tools accelerate marketing execution dramatically — brief-to-copy in seconds, positioning-canvas-to-landing-page in minutes. Ritson's discipline is the corrective to the failure mode this acceleration produces: execution without diagnosis, tactics without strategy, channel decisions before objectives are set. The `pre-launch-marketing-audit` uses Ritson's diagnose-then-strategy-then-tactics sequence as its structuring logic. When the audit flags a launch plan as "tactics looking for a strategy," Ritson is the citation. AI can produce tactics faster than most teams can evaluate them; Ritson's discipline ensures evaluation happens before execution.

## Key concepts the role pulls from

- **Diagnose → Strategy → Tactics.** Ritson's three-phase marketing planning sequence, non-negotiable in order. Diagnosis: understand the market, the customer, the competition. Strategy: choose who to target, what position to take, what objectives to set. Tactics: only then choose channels, messages, and executions. Inverting the order — choosing tactics first because they're exciting — is the canonical Ritson failure mode. Powers the `pre-launch-marketing-audit`'s structural check: does a strategy exist before tactics are selected?
- **Objectives must be dual.** Ritson's recurring teaching: marketing objectives must include both brand (long-term mental availability, loyalty, positioning) and activation (short-term response, conversion, sales) components. Plans that are activation-only are sub-optimal; plans that are brand-only can't be measured. The `launch-brief`'s objective-setting section is grounded in this discipline.
- **ICP is not a segment.** Ritson is consistently critical of the "ideal customer profile" as used in B2B SaaS — it is typically too narrow, conflating the best current customers with the total addressable market. The audience question requires empirical segmentation, not persona fiction. Powers the `positioning-canvas`'s segment-evidence requirement and the `pre-launch-marketing-audit`'s segment-validity check.
- **Creative quality is a multiplier.** Ritson's position contra the data-first school: channel and targeting decisions are less important than creative quality, and the industry systematically underinvests in creative relative to media. AI-generated creative is the lowest-quality creative at scale; Ritson's multiplier frame makes this a strategic problem, not an aesthetic one. Powers the `launch-brief`'s creative-quality review.
- **Marketing is the most misunderstood function.** Ritson's recurring argument: marketing is confused with advertising, confused with social media, and confused with brand design. The actual scope — diagnosis, strategy, targeting, positioning, objectives, tactics, measurement — is broader. This matters for the methodology because the marketing-expert reviewer's role is the full scope, not just copy or channel.

## What to actually read (1-hour budget)

- Marketing Week columns archive — Ritson's most important columns are indexed at marketingweek.com; search for "marketing strategy," "brand vs activation," and "segmentation." The columns are 600-1200 words each; 8-10 columns ~1 hour. This is the most efficient path to the voice and frame.
- Mini MBA in Marketing modules 1-3 (available via public previews or recorded excerpts) — Diagnosis, Strategy, Targeting. These three modules are the load-bearing framework content.

## What to skip

- The Mini MBA's later modules on specific tactics (digital, CRM, pricing) — they apply the framework to surfaces that change faster than the framework does. The framework is the durable asset; the tactic-specific content ages.
- Ritson's brand-valuation content — technically interesting but rarely load-bearing for the role's skills as scoped in Hornero.

## How this anchors role skills

- [`pre-launch-marketing-audit`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/pre-launch-marketing-audit/SKILL.md) — Ritson is the primary structural anchor for the audit's strategy-readiness check. The audit's diagnose-strategy-tactics sequence mirrors Ritson's planning framework directly. A launch plan that leads with tactics — channel mix, posting schedule, ad creative — without a documented strategy is the audit's canonical "Hold" recommendation.
- [`launch-brief`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/launch-brief/SKILL.md) — the brief's objective-setting section (dual brand + activation objectives) and segment-validity check are Ritson's disciplines applied to the brief format.
- [`positioning-canvas`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/positioning-canvas/SKILL.md) — Ritson's segmentation rigour reinforces the canvas's evidence requirement for best-fit customer segments: segments must be empirically evidenced, not persona-based.
- [`strategic-narrative`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/strategic-narrative/SKILL.md) — strategy-before-narrative is Ritson's posture; a narrative written before positioning and strategy are set is premature. The `strategic-narrative` skill's Step 1 (confirm positioning is solid) is Ritson's discipline applied.

## Cross-role connection

Ritson's strategy-before-execution posture is the marketing-function equivalent of the product-manager role's "understand the problem before building the solution" discipline. Both roles insist that moving to tactics/execution before the strategic frame is set produces waste — expensive waste in marketing, shipped-but-wrong features in product. The PM reviewer and the marketing-expert reviewer tend to converge on this critique in `/review` sessions on launch plans: "you have a channel mix but no stated objectives" is the same failure mode as "you have a feature list but no stated customer job." Pair this note with `research/product-manager/` entries on strategic framing when working across both roles on launch readiness.
