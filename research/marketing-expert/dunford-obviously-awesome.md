# April Dunford — distilled

> *Obviously Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning So Customers Get It, Buy It, Love It* (2019) and *Sales Pitch: How to Craft a Story to Stand Out and Win* (2023). The contemporary positioning canon for B2B tech.

## Why it matters for AI-coding

Generic AI-generated positioning produces "for everyone, against nothing" copy — exactly the failure mode Dunford diagnoses as the default state when teams haven't done the work. Her framework forces positioning to be a *deliberate act* with falsifiable inputs (competitive alternatives, unique attributes, the value those enable, best-fit customer segments) rather than a marketing artifact generated from feature lists.

The methodology's `positioning-canvas` skill is, structurally, Dunford's framework operationalised — the components are her components, the evidence base (15-30 interviews + sales-call recordings + win-loss analysis) is her prescription, the anti-positioning self-check is her discipline. When the marketing-expert reviewer flags a positioning draft as "category-validating mush," the citation is Dunford. When the same reviewer flags a launch brief as "positioned for everyone," the citation is Dunford.

## Key concepts the role pulls from

- **Positioning as a deliberate act.** Positioning is the strategic answer to "who is this for, what is it for, what does it stand against." It is *not* a tagline, *not* a category statement, *not* a feature list. AI-generated marketing artifacts default to the latter three; the methodology resists that default by anchoring on Dunford. Powers the `positioning-canvas` skill's entire structure.
- **Competitive alternatives.** Not "competitors" (which presumes you're in the same category) but "what would the customer do if you didn't exist." Includes do-nothing, in-house tools, spreadsheets, status-quo workarounds. Dunford's key move: competitive alternatives are usually broader and weirder than the team thinks. Powers the canvas's first component.
- **Unique attributes.** Provable, distinct features of your product. Dunford is strict: "best in class" is not an attribute (unprovable); "30-second setup" is (testable). Powers the canvas's second component.
- **Value.** The benefit the unique attributes enable. The chain is `attribute → value`, not `value → audience`. AI-generated copy frequently inverts this (claims the value first; the attributes don't support it). Powers the canvas's third component.
- **Best-fit customer segments.** Positioning is per-segment, not universal. A product can be positioned differently for SMB vs. enterprise without contradiction; the segment is part of the positioning, not adjacent to it. Powers the canvas's segments component.
- **Anti-positioning.** What you are *not* — the categories you are deliberately not in, the customer segments you are deliberately not serving. Dunford's underrated move: explicit anti-positioning sharpens what remains. Powers the canvas's anti-positioning self-check.
- **The sales-pitch as operationalised positioning.** (*Sales Pitch*, 2023.) A 4-part structure (Insight / Alternatives / Vision / Magic Gift) that turns the positioning canvas into a 15-minute live pitch. Powers the `strategic-narrative` skill's structure indirectly.

## What to actually read (1-hour budget)

- *Obviously Awesome* — full read, ~150 pages, ~3 hours of unhurried reading but ~1 hour if you're skimming. Dunford writes tight; nothing in the book is padding. Read all of it.
- If you have a second hour: *Sales Pitch* — same author, ~150 pages. Skip if you're not in B2B SaaS or selling to procurement; otherwise read for the live-pitch operationalisation.

## What to skip

- Dunford's older blog posts on positioning that predate the canvas — the framework crystallised in the book; the older posts are version-1 drafts.
- The "10 positioning consultants you should follow on LinkedIn" listicle-style content her brand has accumulated — operational tax, not load-bearing.

- [`pre-launch-marketing-audit`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/pre-launch-marketing-audit/SKILL.md) — the audit's positioning-readiness check is a Dunford pass.

## Cross-role connection

Dunford's competitive-alternatives concept maps onto PM's `jobs-to-be-done` "what would the customer hire instead" question — both refuse to define competition narrowly. Dunford's "evidence base" (15-30 interviews + sales recordings + win-loss) parallels the data-scientist's "is this claim evidenced or vibes" stance. When `/orchestrate` composes a launch readiness review and the marketing-expert reviewer flags positioning evidence as thin, the PM and data-scientist reviewers usually concur — the citations differ but the stance is the same.
