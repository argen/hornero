# Joanna Wiebe — distilled

> Copy Hackers (copyhackers.com, 2011–present) and the voice-of-customer conversion-copy methodology. The discipline of writing copy from customer language rather than company language.

## Why it matters for AI-coding

AI-generated copy reflects the language of the product brief, the feature list, and the marketing team — it is, structurally, inside-out copy. Wiebe's voice-of-customer methodology is the outside-in corrective: mine what customers actually say (in reviews, support tickets, sales calls, interview transcripts) and bring that language into the copy. The gap between company language and customer language is precisely the gap AI generation fills with plausible-sounding approximations that no real customer ever used to describe their problem. The `launch-brief` skill's copy-research requirement and the `pre-launch-marketing-audit`'s customer-language check are Wiebe's methodology operationalised.

## Key concepts the role pulls from

- **Voice-of-customer (VoC) mining.** The core practice: before writing a word of copy, mine customer language from reviews (Amazon, G2, Capterra, app stores), support tickets, sales call recordings, and interview transcripts. Copy that converts uses the exact words and phrases customers use to describe their problems and desired outcomes — not synonyms, not marketer paraphrasing. Powers the `launch-brief`'s copy-research step and the audit's customer-language check.
- **Jobs-aware copy.** Wiebe's copy is organised around the job the customer is hiring the product to do — the functional job, the emotional job, the social job. This maps directly onto Christensen's JTBD framework and Levitt's customer-need frame: the copy names the job first, the product second. Powers the `launch-brief`'s copy structure and the `positioning-canvas`'s voice-of-customer validation step.
- **The awareness ladder.** Wiebe adapts Eugene Schwartz's five levels of awareness (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware) to structure copy: the entry point and the argument depend on where the reader starts on the awareness ladder. A reader who is problem-aware but not solution-aware needs different copy than a reader who is comparing alternatives. Powers the `launch-brief`'s audience-readiness check.
- **Copy from reviews.** Wiebe's most-cited practical technique: search Amazon and G2 reviews for competitor products (or your own) in the category. The reviews that get 3-4 stars often contain the most honest language about what the product does and doesn't deliver — this is the copy a landing page should use, almost verbatim. The `launch-brief` mandates this step for any high-stakes copy surface.
- **Test the message, not the colour.** Wiebe's emphasis: conversion experiments should test message-level hypotheses (different value prop, different framing, different awareness-level entry) rather than design details (button colour, hero image). Message-level tests produce learnings; design-level tests produce noise. The `pre-launch-marketing-audit`'s experimentation check uses this to flag underpowered or message-neutral tests.

## What to actually read (1-hour budget)

- Copy Hackers blog — "How to Do Market Research for Copywriters" and the "Voice of Customer Data" series at copyhackers.com. ~45 minutes for the concentrated methodology.
- "10x Emails" course introduction (free sections at Copy Hackers) — the awareness ladder application to email sequence design. ~15 minutes.
- Wiebe's "Conversion Copywriting 101" YouTube series — if you learn better from video; equivalent content to the blog series.

## What to skip

- The Copy Hackers course library beyond the VoC and awareness-ladder content — there are dozens of courses covering specific formats (SaaS landing pages, email sequences, sales pages). They apply the methodology to specific surfaces; the methodology itself is in the free blog content.
- Guest posts on the Copy Hackers blog — quality varies widely; Wiebe's own writing is the load-bearing source.

## How this anchors role skills

- [`launch-brief`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/launch-brief/SKILL.md) — load-bearing for copy discipline. The brief's copy-research requirement (VoC mining before writing) and customer-language check (does the copy use customer words or company words?) are Wiebe's methodology. The marketing-expert reviewer applies the "did anyone mine reviews for this?" test to copy sections.
- [`pre-launch-marketing-audit`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/pre-launch-marketing-audit/SKILL.md) — the audit's customer-language check and message-testing readiness check derive from Wiebe. The audit flags copy that reads like a feature list rather than a customer's words.
- [`positioning-canvas`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/positioning-canvas/SKILL.md) — the canvas's best-fit customer component benefits from VoC data: the language customers use to describe their situation is the input to both the canvas and the copy.

## Cross-role connection

Wiebe's voice-of-customer discipline and the product-manager role's `discovery-interview-prep` skill are methodological cousins: both are the discipline of using customer language rather than company language, and both require going to primary sources rather than inferring from product briefs. The PM gathers customer language in discovery interviews; Wiebe mines it from existing public sources (reviews, tickets). The outputs — actual customer vocabulary for problems, desired outcomes, and alternative framings — feed both the canvas and the copy. When the PM reviewer and the marketing-expert reviewer both flag a launch brief for company-language copy, the constructive fix requires both disciplines: interview prep for live language mining and VoC mining for existing-record language. Pair this note with `research/product-manager/` discovery entries when working across both roles.
