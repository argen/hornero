# Andy Raskin — distilled

> "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen" (Medium, 2016) and the strategic-narrative consulting methodology published at andyraskin.com. The contemporary template for narrative pitch decks and strategic-launch stories in B2B tech.

## Why it matters for AI-coding

AI-generated launch decks default to feature-list theatre: here is what the product does, here is who it's for, here are the logos. The 5-part narrative Raskin formalised forces a coherent strategic story that starts outside the product and arrives at the product as a necessary consequence of a prior argument. Without the narrative frame, AI-generated decks present the company as if the world was already waiting for it. The `strategic-narrative` skill is Raskin's framework operationalised as a step-by-step procedural; the marketing-expert reviewer flags any launch story that opens with the product rather than the world.

## Key concepts the role pulls from

- **The 5-part structure — in order.** (1) Name the shift. (2) Show winners and losers. (3) Tease the promised land. (4) Introduce capabilities. (5) Present evidence. The order is load-bearing: capability-first is the failure mode Raskin diagnoses in almost every company narrative he reviews. Powers the `strategic-narrative` skill's entire procedure.
- **Shift-first, product-second.** The shift is external — something happening in the world the audience already experiences. By starting with the shift, the narrative earns the audience's attention on their own terms before asking them to care about the product. The product enters only at step four, after the stakes are established.
- **The promised land is specific.** Raskin's teaching: the promised land that can't be pictured doesn't convert. "Move faster" fails; "close the books in two days instead of ten" succeeds. The `strategic-narrative` skill's pictureability check is directly Raskin's discipline.
- **Capability, not product, at step four.** The buyer needs capabilities to reach the promised land. The product is one capability among several — some the buyer already has, some they must acquire from elsewhere. Making the product the only path reads as marketing; making it one of several paths reads as insight.
- **Specificity beats abstraction throughout.** Raskin's repeated teaching in published essays: abstract narratives ("AI is changing everything") are forgettable; specific narratives (name the specific change, the specific stakes, the specific outcome) are retellable. The retell test is Raskin's validation standard.

## What to actually read (1-hour budget)

- "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen" — free on Medium; ~15 minutes. This is the primary source. The annotated breakdown of Zuora's deck is the clearest single illustration of the 5-part structure.
- Andy Raskin's essays at andyraskin.com — especially "Strategic Narrative and the Limits of Category Design" and the narrative-validation essays. Budget ~45 minutes across 3-4 pieces.
- If you have a second hour: *April Dunford's Sales Pitch* (2023) applies Raskin's narrative structure to the B2B sales meeting format — the best operationalisation of the same framework for a live pitch rather than a deck.

## What to skip

- Conference talks of the Medium essay — they add anecdotes but the written essay is more precise. Watch the talk only if you need a demonstration for stakeholder buy-in.
- Third-party "Raskin framework" summaries on LinkedIn and growth-hacking blogs — they invariably simplify the order wrong, making step three and step four interchangeable. Go to the source.

## How this anchors role skills

- [`strategic-narrative`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/strategic-narrative/SKILL.md) — Raskin is the primary load-bearing anchor. The skill's procedure (steps 1-11), the validation checks (8 checks), and the example narrative are all Raskin's framework. Donald Miller and April Dunford are supporting; Raskin is the backbone.
- [`launch-brief`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/launch-brief/SKILL.md) — launches use the strategic narrative as their opening frame. The brief requires a linked narrative canvas; the narrative's structure is Raskin's. A launch brief without a narrative is the `launch-brief` skill's canonical failure mode.
- [`positioning-canvas`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/positioning-canvas/SKILL.md) — the narrative is downstream of positioning; the canvas is upstream. The marketing-expert reviewer will flag a narrative that doesn't trace to an approved canvas — the connection is Raskin's own requirement that the shift be grounded in the company's real positioning.
- [`pre-launch-marketing-audit`](../../roles/marketing-expert/skills/pre-launch-marketing-audit/SKILL.md) — the audit's narrative-readiness check applies Raskin's 5-part structure as a checklist: does a narrative exist? Does it open with the shift? Is the promised land specific? Is evidence named?

## Cross-role connection

Raskin's "promised land" maps directly onto the product-manager role's `cross-functional-orchestration` customer-job framing: both describe a future state the customer is trying to reach, and both make the product a means rather than the destination. The PM reviewer and the marketing-expert reviewer tend to converge on launch plans that lack a clear promised land — the PM calls it a missing customer job; the marketing-expert calls it a missing narrative step three. Pair this note with `research/product-manager/` entries on cross-functional orchestration when working on a launch that spans both roles.
