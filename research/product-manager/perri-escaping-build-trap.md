# Melissa Perri — distilled

> *Escaping the Build Trap: How Effective Product Management Creates Real Value* (2018). Product Institute. The operational diagnosis of why product organizations ship more and more while delivering less and less value — and the structural moves that fix it.

## Why it matters for AI-coding

AI doesn't solve the build trap — it accelerates into it. When a team can ship features in a fraction of the previous time, the pressure to ship *more* intensifies, and the discipline to ask "what outcome does this produce?" weakens. Perri's central argument — that output velocity is not the same as value creation — is more urgent when the output side becomes essentially free.

The PM who hasn't absorbed Perri will treat AI as a throughput multiplier and congratulate the team on shipping faster. The PM who has absorbed Perri will ask: are we moving the metric that matters, or are we generating more activity that doesn't compound?

## Key concepts the role pulls from

- **The build trap.** Organizations that equate shipping features with making progress. The trap is self-reinforcing: customers ask for features, teams ship them, output is high, nobody asks whether usage or outcomes changed. Perri's diagnosis of the build trap is the load-bearing argument behind why `prd` requires success metrics and counter-metrics, not just a feature list.
- **Outputs vs. outcomes.** Outputs are things shipped. Outcomes are changes in customer behavior or business state. The distinction is shared with Cagan and Torres, but Perri gives it the clearest operational framing: "a good product manager is responsible for outcomes, not outputs." Every `prd` success-metric must be an outcome metric.
- **Product-org architecture.** Perri argues that the build trap is structural: organizations that treat PMs as order-takers (the "roadmap translator" archetype) inevitably build traps. Organizations that give PMs outcome mandates escape it. The architectural diagnosis gives the PM language for escalating when the role is being collapsed.
- **The product kata.** Perri's adaptation of Toyota kata to product work: current state → target condition → obstacle → experiment → learn → repeat. A weekly learning cadence, not a quarterly planning cycle. Aligns with Torres' continuous-discovery discipline; the two work in parallel for different loops (hypothesis-testing vs. customer-touchpoint).
- **Three product management archetypes.** The "roadmap translator" (bad), the "mini-CEO" (misunderstood but contains the right aspiration), and the "outcome-owner" (what Perri argues for). Useful as a critique frame when diagnosing what kind of PM role a team is actually operating.

## What to actually read (1-hour budget)

- **Chapters 1-3** — the build trap defined; the three PM archetypes; the case that product management is the fix. The conceptual core of the book. ~55 pages.
- **Chapter 7** — the product kata. The operational loop that turns "we want outcomes" from a slogan into a practice. 15 pages, high density.
- **Chapter 12** — product-org architecture for escaping the trap at the organizational level. Read if you're ever in a conversation about how to structure a product team, not just how to operate within one.

## What to skip

- Chapters 8-11 cover product strategy, roadmaps, and OKRs at a level that Wodtke and Cagan cover with more precision. Perri's treatment is valuable for organizational context; for operating PM, defer to the more surgical sources.
- The later chapters on transforming large organizations are worth a skim but contain a lot of change-management process that's tangential for an individual PM or an agent adopting the role.

## How this anchors role skills

- [`prd`](../../skills/prd/SKILL.md) — Perri's outputs-vs-outcomes distinction is the load-bearing argument for why the `prd` skill requires outcome-shaped success metrics. "Ship feature X" as a success criterion is a build-trap artifact.
- [`cross-functional-orchestration`](../../skills/cross-functional-orchestration/SKILL.md) — the "outcomes over outputs" synthesis principle in Step 5 cites Perri alongside Wodtke. The "what outcomes will tell us this decision was right?" question is Perri's product kata made explicit.
- [`okrs`](../../skills/okrs/SKILL.md) — Perri's distinction reinforces why OKR Key Results must be outcome metrics, not output milestones. Output-shaped KRs are build-trap signals.

## Cross-role connection

Perri's build-trap diagnosis and the tester role's "test coverage doesn't mean correctness" anti-pattern are the same epistemological mistake in adjacent domains: both confuse the measurable proxy (features shipped, tests passing) with the underlying goal (customer value, behavior correctness). The multi-role-review's insistence on counter-metrics echoes both.
