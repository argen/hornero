# Marketing expert — sources

The curated bibliography for the marketing-expert role. Skills cite from this list. New frameworks introduced in skills must trace to a name here.

## Anchors

### April Dunford — Ambient Strategy
- *Obviously Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning So Customers Get It, Buy It, Love It* (2019)
- *Sales Pitch: How to Craft a Story to Stand Out and Win* (2023)
- Positioning canvas (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → market category → who-cares-most)
- **Why she matters:** Contemporary canon on B2B positioning; sharpest practitioner voice on why bad positioning kills good products.
- Used in: positioning-canvas, sales-pitch, category-design skills.

### Theodore Levitt — Harvard Business School
- "Marketing Myopia" (HBR, 1960; reprinted 2004)
- *The Marketing Imagination* (1983)
- **Why he matters:** The foundational warning — companies define themselves by what they make rather than what their customers buy. The JTBD lineage starts here.
- Used in: market-definition, business-redefinition skills.

### David Ogilvy — Ogilvy & Mather
- *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983)
- *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (1963)
- **Why he matters:** Canonical advertising craft — testing-driven copy, the "long copy works" thesis, the discipline of writing for the reader, not the client.
- Used in: ad-craft, long-copy, headline-design skills.

### Byron Sharp — Ehrenberg-Bass Institute
- *How Brands Grow* (2010)
- *How Brands Grow: Part 2* (2015, with Jenni Romaniuk)
- Mental availability + physical availability + distinctive brand assets
- The "double jeopardy" law; light-buyer importance
- **Why he matters:** Evidence-based marketing — empirical patterns across markets that contradict much industry intuition (e.g., loyalty programs don't drive growth; mental availability does).
- Used in: brand-growth, distinctive-assets, share-of-mind skills.

### Andy Raskin — strategic narrative consultant
- "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen" (Medium, 2016)
- The 5-part strategic-narrative structure: Name the Shift → Show Winners/Losers → Tease the Promised Land → Introduce Capabilities → Present Evidence
- **Why he matters:** The contemporary template for narrative pitch decks; widely adopted in tech.
- Used in: strategic-narrative, sales-deck skills.

### Rory Sutherland — Ogilvy UK
- *Alchemy: The Surprising Power of Ideas That Don't Make Sense* (2019)
- Behavioral economics applied to marketing
- The "psycho-logic" of consumer choice
- **Why he matters:** Counter to over-rational marketing thinking; surfaces the role of perception, framing, and irrationality in purchase decisions.
- Used in: psychological-framing, perceived-value skills.

### Bob Hoffman — The Ad Contrarian
- *Marketers Are From Mars, Consumers Are From New Jersey* (2015)
- *BadMen: How Advertising Went From a Minor Annoyance to a Major Menace* (2017)
- **Why he matters:** Sharpest critic of digital-marketing dogma; counter-conventional voice on attribution, programmatic, brand-vs-performance.
- Used in: marketing-skepticism, channel-evaluation skills.

### Joanna Wiebe — Copy Hackers
- Conversion copywriting methodology
- "Voice of customer" data-driven copy
- Message-mining from review sites, support tickets, sales calls
- **Why she matters:** Practical methodology for copy that converts — using actual customer language rather than marketer language.
- Used in: voice-of-customer, conversion-copy skills.

### Mark Ritson — Marketing Week, Mini MBA
- "Marketing Mind" columns
- Strategic-then-tactical marketing planning (diagnose → strategy → tactics)
- **Why he matters:** Working-strategist voice on marketing rigor; pushes back on both "creative-first" and "data-first" extremes in favor of strategic-then-creative.
- Used in: strategic-planning, brief-design skills.

### Donald Miller — StoryBrand
- *Building a StoryBrand: Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen* (2017)
- The StoryBrand SB7 framework (hero, problem, guide, plan, call to action, success/failure stakes)
- **Why he matters:** Accessible, widely-adopted narrative framework for brand messaging; especially strong for small-team marketing without specialist staff.
- Used in: brand-narrative, website-messaging skills.

## See also

- **Eugene Schwartz** — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) — the "five awareness levels" framework for matching message to audience readiness.
- **Philip Kotler** — *Marketing Management* — the academic canon; references for vocabulary.
- **Lenny Rachitsky** — [lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/) — contemporary B2B growth canon.
- **Bob Moesta** — JTBD applied to demand-side sales and marketing.
- **Peep Laja & Wynter** — message testing and ICP research methodology.
- **Ann Handley** — *Everybody Writes* (2014) — practical content/copy guidance.
- **Claude Hopkins** — *Scientific Advertising* (1923) — testing-driven advertising; historical reference.

## Curation notes

Cap is ~10 anchors plus "see also." Adding a new anchor requires demoting an existing one.

The marketing role spans positioning, brand, performance, narrative, copy, and strategy. Anchor selection covers each pillar: Dunford (positioning), Levitt (market definition), Ogilvy (advertising craft), Sharp (evidence-based growth), Raskin (narrative), Sutherland (behavioral), Hoffman (skepticism), Wiebe (conversion copy), Ritson (strategic planning), Miller (small-team narrative).

Notable gap by design: no martech / attribution tool authors. Tools change fast; tools are not frameworks of thought. Skills can mention tools; they shouldn't drive the conceptual scaffolding.

JTBD overlaps with product-manager role; Bob Moesta appears in both "see also" lists deliberately. Positioning (Dunford, Moore) bridges the two roles.
